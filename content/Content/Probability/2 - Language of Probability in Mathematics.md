
In mathematics, probability is a theoretical measure of the likelihood of (an) event(s) occurring, shown as a number 0 - impossible - to 1 - certain. 

*Note: Therefore, probability can NOT be higher than 1.*

## Notations:

- The mathematical **notation** representing the probability of an event A is written as follow: 

==**Pr(A) or P(A)**==

Conventionally, event A' represents the non-occurrence of event A.

	Therefore, we have a method: 

==**Pr(A) = 1 - Pr(A')==
==Pr(A') = 1 - Pr(A)==**

## The intersection of event A and event B

**Definition**: The intersection of event A and event B is the set of elements that are common to both. It is written as:

==**P(A ∩ B)**== 

*read as "A intersection B" or "A cap B."*
### Union of A and B

**Definition**: Union of A and B is the set of all elements in both A and B. It is written as:

==**P(A ∪ B)**==

*read as "Union of A and B" or, "A union B."*

## **Example**: 
**Question**: a box there are 5 fruits: an apple, 2 bananas, and 2 strawberries. When a person randomly picks up one fruit from that box.
	a) What is the possibility that fruit is apple? 
	b) What is the possibility that fruit is NOT apple?
**Solution**:
	There are 5 items in that box.
	Assume that 
	Pr(A) illustrates that that person chooses apple. 
	a) 
	Pr(A) = **number of apple / total number of all the items in the box** = 1 / 5
	b) 
	=> Pr(A') = 1 - (1 / 5) = 4 / 5
